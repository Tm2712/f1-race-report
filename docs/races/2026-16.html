How these findings are calculated

Gaps and running order come from OpenF1 lap start times, which the API describes as approximate, so treat gaps as accurate to a few tenths of a second. A stop counts as an undercut attempt when the car was within 3 s behind another car that had not pitted more recently, and neither stop happened under a safety car. The result is judged two laps after the car ahead pitted, or 10 laps later if it stayed out. The analysis does not know tyre age differences, traffic on rejoining or team intent. Pace uses green-flag laps with in, out and first laps removed and a rough fuel correction of 0.03 s per lap, so small pace gaps are not meaningful.

How the what-if is calculated

A pace model is fitted to every clean lap: fuel-corrected lap time = driver pace + compound offset + wear rate per compound x tyre age + a race-lap trend. For each stop, the model re-times that driver's two stints as if the stop happened up to 8 laps earlier or later, keeps every other driver's actual times, and re-ranks the finish by timing data (which can differ from the official result after penalties). The ± comes from refitting on 100 resampled sets of laps; it understates the true uncertainty because laps are not independent. Picking the best of up to 16 shifts flatters the result, so read gains as upper estimates. The model ignores traffic, rivals reacting, tyre cliffs and any tyre age not seen in the race (those shifts are skipped), stops near safety cars are excluded, as are drivers who did not finish and stops that follow or precede a stint shorter than 8 laps (such as pit-lane starts).
